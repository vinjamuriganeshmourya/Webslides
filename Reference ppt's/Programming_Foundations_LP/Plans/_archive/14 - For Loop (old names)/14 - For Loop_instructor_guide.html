Checkpoint 1 · 14 min · they can say why a list ends a loop. If not, cut the agenda card to one press.

Opening · 1 min · slide 1 of 30

Welcome

How it goes
  1. Let the room settle. Nothing to teach on this card.
  2. Say what today is about in one line: we already know one kind of loop, today we meet the other one, and it is the shorter one.
  3. Hand off: first, a quick check on last session.
  • Press Next as soon as the room is quiet.

Watch for Nobody has a laptop open yet. Say laptops shut once here and it holds until worksheet task 1.

Recap · 4 min · slide 2 of 30

Recap

How it goes
  1. Card 1 of the panel is one word: Recap. The card says nothing else, so you do: last session we did the while loop. Quick recap before we start.
  2. Press once. Question 1 appears and its timer starts by itself.
  3. Read option A, count hands. Then B, then C, then D. Every hand is counted before anything is revealed.
  4. Press Reveal answer. Then say why each wrong option was tempting, not only why the right one is right.
  5. Same for questions 2 and 3. Keep it brisk: read, vote, reveal, move.
  6. Hand off: good. Now put all of that aside for two minutes.
  • Four steps in this card: the word, then three questions.
  • The timers start by themselves here, and only here.
  • Never name a student. Ask for hands, not for names.

Watch for On question 1 most of the room says three. The answer is four. That gap is the whole reason today exists, so let them sit in it for a second.

Hook · 4 min · slide 3 of 30

Hook: Play all

How it goes
  1. Start from what they have: everyone here has a music app on the phone.
  2. Press 1. The playlist. Say nothing clever: just five songs, one list.
  3. Press 2. The tap and the first song. One tap. That is all you did.
  4. Press 3 and 4. Ask before you press 5: who told it to play the next song? Take two answers. Do not correct anyone.
  5. Press 5. The highlight runs to the last song. Let it finish moving.
  6. Press 6 and stop. Ask: nowhere on this screen does it say five. So what told it to stop? Wait. The answer you want is the list ran out.
  7. Press 7. One more song appears. You changed nothing else, and it plays six.
  8. Press 8. The phone fades and the shape behind it is left on screen: a list, one job, and an arrow going back for the next item. Read it out in that order: take a list, do one job, once for each item.
  9. Point at the red line: nowhere does it say six. Hand off: so what is this thing called in Python?
  • Eight presses. Arrow keys step it.
  • Two stops where you ask before pressing: before 5 and on 6.
  • Press 8 is the one that matters. Do not rush it: it is the for loop, drawn, before anyone has said the words.
  • No timer on this card. A clock turns a question into a test.

Watch for Somebody may say the app knows it has five songs. Good answer, take it, then ask how it knew after they added the sixth. Nobody told it anything.

A string is a sequence of characters

How it goes

Can we simplify this?

How it goes

Concept · 2 min · slide 8 of 30

For Loop: the syntax

How it goes
  1. Now the shape: for, a name you choose, in, the sequence, colon.
  2. Read it as English: for each item in this sequence, do the block below.

For Loop over a word

How it goes

Concept · 1 min · slide 10 of 30

What the for loop drops

How it goes
  1. Three lines gone: no start, no check, no change.
  2. Ask: so what decides how many times it runs now? The sequence, and only the sequence.
  3. Hand off: right. Open your worksheet.
  • This card is the payoff of the last three. Do not rush past it.

Activity · 4 min · slide 11 of 30 · worksheet task 1

Trace it before you run it

How it goes
  1. Hand out the worksheet now. One file, they open it and type into it.
  2. Worksheet tab open, playground shut. Say it.
  3. Read the program out loud once. Do not say what it is for.
  4. Fill the first pass row together, out loud: ch is H, n becomes 1, line 5 prints H. Then they do the rest alone.
  5. Start the timer. Five minutes.
  6. Walk the rows and talk while they work: are you done? Any doubts about the task? Two minutes left, finish the last row.
  • Press play on the timer. It does not start by itself.
  • Fill row 1 aloud. Rows 2 and 3 are theirs.
  • Do not correct anybody at their desk. The next card does that.

Watch for Pens down first, then say the check: exactly one of your three pass rows should be empty. Not before they stop typing.

Reveal · 3 min · slide 12 of 30

Now check your own rows

How it goes
  1. Hands off keyboards. Wait for it before the first press.
  2. Press once per operation. Twelve presses. After each one, five seconds of silence while they check that one row on their own screen.
  3. At press 4, say it plainly: the H is gone out of the sequence. It does not come back.
  4. The ch and n boxes keep every value they have held. The old ones are struck through and stay on screen: n was 0, now it is 1. The 0 did not vanish, it was replaced.
  5. At press 10, stop: the loop asks for the next item and there is none. That is how it ends.
  6. At press 11: line 7 goes grey and stays grey. It was never reached.
  7. Hand off: anyone whose row 3 has writing in it, that is the thing to look at.
  • Twelve presses, one operation each. Never two at once.
  • Wait about five seconds after each press.
  • Arrow keys step it.

Watch for A student who filled all three pass rows traced what they expected, not what runs. That is the useful finding, and it is worth naming without naming them.

Explainer · 3 min · slide 13 of 30

Follow the item

How it goes
  1. Pick a word with the buttons. Start with Rain.
  2. Before pressing anything: write down how many lines this prints and what the last line is. Wait for them to write it.
  3. Press Play. The boxes leave the sequence one at a time.
  4. Press Reset, pick nothing, the empty word. Ask: what does this print? Take answers. Many will say an error.
  5. Press Play. Nothing prints from the loop, and line 3 stays grey. An empty sequence means the body never runs. Not once.
  6. Hand off: hold on to that. It comes back in ten minutes.
  • Use One step when the room disagrees. It stops where they argue.
  • The buttons belong to this card. Click them, do not use the arrow keys.
  • Two to three minutes. It is a reveal, not a lesson.

Watch for The empty word is the one that matters. Line 3 staying grey after the run ends is the finding, so do not reset it away too quickly.

Hands-on · 5 min · slide 14 of 30 · worksheet task 2

Type it, then change the sequence

How it goes
  1. Worksheet tab and playground only. Say it.
  2. Step 1: they type the program and run it. Give them two minutes.
  3. Step 2: change the word to Delhi. Write the number first, then run.
  4. Step 3, and frame it as your own idea, not a test: now try an empty word, two quote marks with nothing in between. Write what you think happens first.
  5. Let them react to step 3 before you say anything at all.
  6. Then ask one of the groups what they got. Hand off: so a sequence can be empty, and the loop just does nothing.

Concept · 2 min · slide 15 of 30

Range: starts at 0, stops before n

How it goes
  1. Start from what they just did: a string gave us a sequence for free. What if I want numbers?
  2. Show range. Two facts and no more: it starts at 0, and it stops before the number you give it.
  3. Say stops before twice. It is the half that gets forgotten.
  4. Hand off: so what does range of five actually give?
  • Two facts only. The examples come next.

Watch for Do not say up to n. It is heard as including n, and that is the misconception the rest of the session fights.

How it goes

  2. Show the boxes. Five numbers, and the last one is 4.
  3. Ask: where is 5? It is the stop value. It never comes out.

Predict, then look · 4 min · slide 17 of 30

The range dial

How it goes
  1. Start from the slide they just saw: range of five gave us five numbers. Let us take the lid off.
  2. The dial opens at range(1, 5). Ask, and wait: which numbers come out, and how many? Take two answers.
  3. Press Show the numbers. They pour on to the line one at a time. The stop value gets a red cross on its own tick.
  4. Read the line under it with them: stop minus start is the count, and the last number out is one less than stop.
  5. Now change something. Press + on stop. The numbers hide again, so ask again before you press. Do this three or four times.
  6. Finish on start and stop the same, for example 4 and 4. Nothing comes out at all. Hand off: hold on to that one.
  • Changing either number hides the numbers again. That is deliberate: they predict before every reveal, not only the first one.
  • The buttons belong to this card. Click them.
  • Always end on the case where start and stop are equal.

Watch for Say stops before, never up to. Up to is heard as including the number, and that is the misconception the rest of the session fights.

Machine · 4 min · slide 18 of 30

The range machine

How it goes

  2. Take a show of hands per option, then click the one the room chose. Run stays locked until something is picked.
  3. Press Run it. The numbers pour out one at a time. Read them with the room.
  4. Read the verdict for every option, not only the right one.
  5. Level 5 is the one to slow down on: range of four comma four gives nothing at all. Tie it back to the empty word from the explainer.
  6. Level 6 is the mistake from the deck, found by them rather than shown. Hand off: remember that. You will meet it again in ten minutes.
  • Six levels. Next level is disabled until the current one has run.
  • The buttons belong to this card. Click them.

Predict, then look · 4 min · slide 25 of 30

The missing row

How it goes
  1. Two programs side by side. Read the two loop lines out. One character apart. Plus one.
  2. Ask, and wait: n is four. How many rows does the left one print? And the right one? Take both numbers from the room.
  3. Press Run them both. The star rows land one at a time on each side.
  4. Then the dashed empty row appears on the left, labelled the row you wanted. Say nothing for a moment and let them look at the hole.
  5. Change n with the plus and minus buttons and run it again. The left one is always exactly one row short, whatever n is.

Common guidelines

Never start a card cold

Every card opens from something the room already has: the app on their phone, the program still on their screens, the answer somebody just gave. If a card feels abrupt, the fix is one sentence at the end of the card before it.

Walk it in steps, and check as you go

Do not deliver a finished explanation. Say one step, then ask the next as a question. So what happens when the sequence is empty? Nothing runs, right? Good. If the room does not answer that, you have found the gap for free. Offer doubts often. It costs four seconds.

How every poll runs

Let them think. Read option A and count hands, then B, then C, then D. Every option gets counted before anything is revealed. Then reveal, and explain every option, not only the right one. A wrong option nobody explains is a misconception that goes home with them.

Picking students

Call a group, never a name. Back three rows, what did you get? Rotate the groups so it is not always the front. Never single anyone out for a wrong answer.

When nobody answers

Count ten seconds in your head. Then narrow the question rather than answering it. Just tell me, is it more than three or less than three?

Why the worksheet comes before the keyboard

The playground is where they check, not where they start. A task typed into the playground first and written down afterwards is a transcript, not a prediction, and the whole value of the beat is gone. Say write first, run second every time.

The laptop rule

Laptops shut except on a worksheet or playground beat. The entries say worksheet tab open on the first step of a task card and worksheet closed on the card after it, so it gets said at every change.

The cut order, if you are behind

  • Card 22, range(start, end), to one press. Saves 1 min.
  • Card 5, the agenda, to one press. Saves 1 min.
  • The machine down to levels 1, 5 and 6. Saves 2 min.
  • The takeaways map to three presses. Saves 2 min.
  • Never cut: worksheet task 1, the reveal, the explainer, or the exit question. They are the session.

If they ask

  • Is ch a keyword? No. You choose that name. for x in word works exactly the same.
  • Why does range start at 0? Because positions in Python start at 0. It matches how you index a string.
  • Can I use a while loop instead? Yes, always. Use a for loop when you already have a sequence. Use while when you do not.
  • Does range(4, 4) give an error? No. It gives an empty sequence, and a loop over it simply does not run.
  • What if the word is empty? Same thing. The body never runs, and anything after the loop still runs normally.
  • Can I change the word inside the loop? Leave this for a later session. Say it is possible and that it causes trouble, and move on.
  • Is range(1, n+1) better than range(n) with a plus one? Neither is better. Both are correct. Pick the one you can read.
  • Why is there a space after every star? Because the question asks for it. "* " has a space inside the quote marks.
