Recap · 3 min · card 3 of 32 · in a 4 min block

The while loop, in three parts

How it goes
  1. Three parts of a while loop on the left, three jobs on the right, in a different order. Read the six out loud once.
  2. Ask: which two belong together? Take it as a call-out, not as hands.
  3. Press Join them. The pair joins and one line says why.
  4. Same for pair 2 and pair 3. Read the why line out each time: it is doing the teaching, not the join.
  5. The condition pair is the one to slow down on. Say the count out loud: the question is asked four times, not three, because it is asked once more at the end when the answer is finally no.
  6. Hand off: hold on to those three. Today, two of them disappear.
  • Three presses. The format is match; session 12 used bug, so the room does not meet the same shape twice running.
  • Never name a student. Take call-outs, and let a wrong pair be said out loud without correcting the person who said it.
  • This card is not a warm-up. Card 13 is about which of these three lines the for loop drops, so all three have to be named here.
  • Short of time: join two pairs, not three. The third one is the line today drops anyway.

Watch for Most rooms pair the condition with the starting value. That is the confusion today clears up, so let it be said before you join the right two.

Hook · 4 min · card 4 of 32 · in a 7 min block

One tap, and it keeps going

How it goes
  1. Start from what they have: everyone here has this app open on the way in. It is Spotify, and the playlist is theirs.
  2. Press 1. The playlist. Say nothing clever: five songs, one list.
  3. Press 2. The green button, one tap, and the first song starts. One tap. That is all you did.
  4. Press 3. Song one ends and song two starts, then song three. Ask before you press 4: who told it to play the next song? Take two answers. Do not correct anyone.
  5. Press 4 and stop. The highlight reaches the last song and the bar reads Stopped, the list ended. Ask: nowhere on this screen does it say five. So what told it to stop? Wait. The answer you want is that the list ran out.
  6. Press 5. A sixth song appears and it plays six. You changed nothing else. You never typed a number anywhere.
  7. Press 6. The question lands: where else does one tap keep going by itself until a list runs out? Do not answer it. The next card is where the answers go.
  • Six presses. Arrow keys step it.
  • Two stops where you ask before pressing: before press 4, and on press 4.
  • Tell the whole song story before you ask them anything. A question at press 3 cuts the story in half and the room never sees it end.
  • No timer on this card. A clock turns a question into a test.
  • Short of time: take one answer before press 4 instead of two. Presses 4 and 6 cannot go.

Watch for Somebody may say the app knows it has five songs. Good answer, take it, then ask how it knew after they added the sixth. Nobody told it anything.

Hook · 3 min · card 5 of 32 · in a 7 min block

Where else does this happen?

How it goes
  1. Press 1. The question is at the top and three empty slots are under it, each with its machine already drawn pale inside. This is their turn. Take four answers out loud. Say which slot each one would go in. Do not judge any of them.
  2. Press 2. Our three land and start turning: the attendance register, Instagram stories, the billing counter. Read the one line under each.
  3. Say the shape once, in their words: a list that ends, and one job per item in it.
  4. Press 3. Everything goes and the shape behind all four is left on screen: a list, one job, and an arrow going back for the next item. Read it in that order: take a list, do one job, once for each item.
  5. Press 4. The red line arrives. Point at it: nowhere does it say six. Hand off: so what is this thing called in Python?
  • Four presses.
  • Press 1 is theirs and press 3 is yours. Their examples come out before ours, or ours become the answer and there is nothing left to think about.
  • Press 3 is the one that matters. Do not rush it: it is the for loop, drawn, before anyone has said the words.
  • Short of time: take two answers instead of four and go straight to press 3. Press 3 is the one that cannot go.

Watch for If a group gives an example with a fixed count in it, like ten press-ups, take it and ask what happens when there are eleven. That is the difference this card is drawing.

Concept · 2 min · card 8 of 32 · in a 5 min block

Identify the mistake

How it goes
  1. This is last session's code, not today's. Set it up: we wanted this to print Python, one letter on each line.
  2. Read the expected output on the right, then the actual output. Ask: what is missing? The last letter.
  3. Ask: where is the mistake? Give it thirty seconds and take hands. Point at the line they name, do not confirm it yet.
  4. Hand off: let us see which line it was.
  • Do not give the answer on this card. The next card is the answer.
  • Count the moving parts out loud while they look: a counter, a len, a condition, an index, and a plus one. Five things to get right.
  • Short of time: give it twenty seconds, not thirty, and take one answer.

Watch for Most rooms go for the print line first, because that is where the output comes from. Let that be said. The mistake is one line above it.

Concept · 2 min · card 10 of 32 · in a 5 min block

Can we simplify this?

How it goes

Explainer · 3 min · card 14 of 32

"Python", one character at a time

How it goes
  1. The string is on screen whole, as one thing: "Python".
  2. Ask before you press: is that one thing or six? Take hands for one, then for six.
  3. Press Split it. The string opens into its six characters, left to right. Say the word once, slowly: a sequence.
  4. Ask: how many lines will this print? Take a number from the room before anything runs.
  5. Press One step and walk it. Say what each press proves off the caption. The box the loop takes vanishes: point at that.
  6. Run it again with Go. Two characters, two passes.
  7. Now the one that matters. Pick nothing, the empty word. Ask first: what does this print? Take answers. Most rooms say an error.
  8. Press Play. Line 3 is never reached and it stays grey after the run ends. Only Done prints. Say it: no error, no output, and the program carried on.
  9. Hand off: your turn. Same idea, on paper first.
  • The split is the point of the card. Do not press it for them.
  • Always ask for a number before you press Play. A prediction after the run is not a prediction.
  • The empty word is the state that stays on screen. If you keep one word, keep that one.
  • Short of time: one word, not three, and make it nothing. The empty word is the finding.

Watch for An empty string is an error is the belief this card exists to break. Let it be said out loud before you press Play.

Activity · 7 min · card 15 of 32 · worksheet task 1

Trace it, then check it

How it goes
  1. Press 1 is the task. The program is on the left and the instructions are on the right. Say worksheet tab open, playground shut.
  2. Read the three columns of the table out loud: what ch holds, what n is after line 4, and what line 5 prints.
  3. Start the timer. Five minutes. Walk the room; do not answer, just look at what people have written.
  4. Pens down before anything else happens.
  5. Press 2 and keep going. Each press is one operation and the caption says what it proves. The code stays on the left the whole way, so they can check their own row against the one on screen.
  6. Slow down on the press where the loop asks for a third item and there is none. That is where the program ends, and it is the row most people wrote something in.
  7. The last press prints n. Say the check out loud now, and only now: you should have used two rows, not three.
  8. Hand off: now type one yourself.
  • Eleven presses on one card. Press 1 is the task; presses 2 to 11 are the answer.
  • Hand the worksheet out here, on this card, and nowhere else.
  • Do not press past 1 until pens are down. The answer is on the same card as the question, which is the point and also the risk.
  • Short of time: give three minutes, not five, and walk the last four presses only.

Watch for Three rows filled in instead of two. That is the misconception of the whole session, and it is worth the extra thirty seconds.

Hands-on · 5 min · card 16 of 32 · worksheet task 2

Type it, then change the sequence

How it goes
  1. Playground open now, and the worksheet beside it. Nothing else.
  2. Step 1: type it exactly and run it. Four letters, four lines, then Done.
  3. Step 2: change the word to Delhi. Write the number of lines first, then run.
  4. Step 3: change the word to two quote marks with nothing between them. Write what it will print first, then run.
  5. Take a show of hands on step 3 before anyone runs it: who thinks it errors? Then let them run it.
  6. Hand off: quiz time. Same idea, on the portal.
  • Say write first, run second at every step. It is the whole value of the beat.
  • Five minutes is enough for all three if you start the timer straight away.
  • Short of time: drop the Delhi change. Typing it once and emptying it are the parts they cannot skip.

Watch for Students who type print(word) instead of print(ch) get the whole word on one line. Good mistake: ask them which one the loop changes every pass.

Activity · 4 min · card 22 of 32 · in a 9 min block

The range dial

How it goes
  1. range(1, 5) is on screen and the number line runs from 0 to 10. Nothing is marked yet.
  2. Ask: which numbers come out, and how many? Take both answers out loud before pressing.
  3. Press Show the numbers. They land one at a time, start is tagged, and stop gets a cross through it on its own tick.
  4. Read the summing line: stop minus start is how many come out.
  5. Change the stop to 8 and ask again. Changing a number hides the answer, so they predict every time, not only the first.
  6. Now the one worth the minute: set start and stop to the same number. Ask: what comes out? Then press.
  7. Say the finding: nothing comes out, and a loop over nothing simply does not run. Tie it to the empty word on card 14.
  8. Hand off: now a real question.
  • Four settings is the most this card can take: the default, one change, the equal case, and one of theirs.
  • Ask both questions every time. Which numbers is the easy half; how many is the half that gets the pattern right later.
  • Short of time: show the default, then jump straight to start and stop equal. Skip the middle settings.

Watch for range(1, 5) gives five numbers is the answer to expect. The cross on the stop tick is there so nobody has to take your word for it.

Concept · 2 min · card 23 of 32 · in a 6 min block

The pattern question

How it goes
  1. Read the question and the sample input and output. Nothing else.
  2. Ask: how many rows for an input of 4? Four. How many stars on the last row? Four.
  3. Say the shape of the answer without giving it: one row per pass, and the number of stars changes every pass.

Activity · 4 min · card 26 of 32 · in a 8 min block

Which one is right?

How it goes
  1. Two programs, side by side, A and B. They start at 1 either way. Read both loop lines out loud.
  2. Ask: which one prints all n rows? Hands up for A. Count them. Hands up for B. Count them. Say both numbers out loud.
  3. Do not hint. A wrong majority here is worth more than a right one.
  4. Press Run them both. The rows land on each side and the counts arrive under them.
  5. The dashed empty row appears on the left: the row you wanted. Point at it and wait.
  6. Say the rule once: range stops before the stop value, so range(1, n) never reaches n.
  7. Change n and run it again. The gap is one row, every time, for every n.
  8. Hand off: so what do you write instead?
  • Count both hands before you press. The vote is the reason the gap lands.
  • Two runs at most: n = 4, then one value the room picks.
  • Short of time: run it once at n = 4 and stop. Do not change n.

Watch for After the run, ask what n would make the two agree. There is none. That is worth saying out loud.

  6. Hand off: one map, and we are done.

Common guidelines

Never start a card cold

Every card opens from something the room already has: the app on their phone, the program still on their screens, the answer somebody just gave. If a card feels abrupt, the fix is one sentence at the end of the card before it.

Walk it in steps, and check as you go

Do not deliver a finished explanation. Say one step, then ask the next as a question. So what happens when the sequence is empty? Nothing runs, right? Good. If the room does not answer that, you have found the gap for free. Offer doubts often. It costs four seconds.

How every poll runs

Let them think. Read option A and count hands, then B, then C, then D. Every option gets counted before anything is revealed. Then reveal, and explain every option, not only the right one. A wrong option nobody explains is a misconception that goes home with them.

Picking students

Call a group, never a name. Back three rows, what did you get? Rotate the groups so it is not always the front. Never single anyone out for a wrong answer.

When nobody answers

Count ten seconds in your head. Then narrow the question rather than answering it. Just tell me, is it more than three or less than three?

Why the worksheet comes before the keyboard

The playground is where they check, not where they start. A task typed into the playground first and written down afterwards is a transcript, not a prediction, and the whole value of the beat is gone. Say write first, run second every time.

The laptop rule

Laptops shut except on a worksheet, playground or quiz beat. The entries say worksheet tab open on the first step of a task card and worksheet closed on the card after it, so it gets said at every change.

The cut order, if you are behind

  • Card 7, the plan, to two presses. Saves 1 min.
  • Card 20, range(5), out entirely. The dial says it better. Saves 1 min.

If they ask

  • Is ch a keyword? No. You choose that name. for x in word works exactly the same.
  • Why does range start at 0? Because positions in Python start at 0. It matches how you index a string.
  • Can I use a while loop instead? Yes, always. Use a for loop when you already have a sequence. Use while when you do not.
  • Does range(4, 4) give an error? No. It gives an empty sequence, and a loop over it simply does not run.
  • What if the word is empty? Same thing. The body never runs, and anything after the loop still runs normally.
  • Can I change the word inside the loop? Leave this for a later session. Say it is possible and that it causes trouble, and move on.
  • Is range(1, n+1) better than range(n) with a plus one? Neither is better. Both are correct. Pick the one you can read.
  • Why is there a space after every star? Because the question asks for it. "* " has a space inside the quote marks.
  • Can a for loop go over numbers without range? Yes, over a list, which is a later session. Today range is the one that makes a sequence of numbers.
