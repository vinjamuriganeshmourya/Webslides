Recap · 3 min · card 3 of 32 · in a 4 min block

What range(2, 6) hands you

How it goes
  1. Five numbers along the top, two bins under them. Read the five out loud once.
  2. Ask, one number at a time: does this one come out of range(2, 6), yes or no? Count the hands for yes, then count the hands for no, then press Drop it. Every number, every time.
  3. The two to slow down on are 5 and 6. Read the why line out loud on both.
  4. Say the whole sequence once at the end: 2, 3, 4, 5. Four numbers. The start is in, the stop is not.
  5. Hand off: hold on to that. Everything we do today stops one place early, in exactly the same way.
  • Five presses. The format is sort. Session 12 used bug and session 14 used match, so the room does not meet the same shape twice running.
  • Count both options aloud for every chip before you press.
  • Never name a student. Take a show of hands, not a call-out.
  • Short of time: drop three chips, keep 5 and 6. Those two are the rule.

Watch for The hands for 6 are the read you want. If a third of the room says yes, slow down: that same mistake costs them the exit question.

Hook · 4 min · card 4 of 32

The message the search cannot find

How it goes
  1. Start from something every one of them has done: somebody in your group types like this. Point at the phone.
  2. Press 1. The chat. Ask: what does that message say? The room says SORRY in about a second. Take it.
  3. Press 2. The search bar opens with SORRY typed in it and the chat says No messages found. Wait. Let them see that the letters are right there on the screen. Ask: why can it not find it?
  4. Take two or three answers. Do not correct anybody. The boxes on the right are the answer: nine boxes, not five. Four of them are spaces, and a space is a character like any other.
  5. Ask: so which boxes are the letters in? Wait for 0, 2, 4, 6, 8.
  6. Press 3. Five marks land on boxes 0 to 4, side by side, and the box on the right fills with S O R. Say: a run of characters, one after another. That is the only shape we have ever asked for, and it drags the spaces along.
  7. Ask before you press 4: so what would I have to change? Take two answers.
  8. Press 4 and stop talking while it moves. The marks stretch out onto 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8, the letters light up, and the answer becomes SORRY. Then say the line: same first box. It just jumps two at a time now.
  • Four presses. Arrow keys step it.
  • Three stops where you ask before pressing: presses 1, 2 and 4.
  • Do not use the words slice, step or method here. The room names the topic on the next card.
  • No timer on this card. A clock turns a question into a test.
  • Short of time: take one answer at press 2 instead of three. Presses 3 and 4 cannot go.

Watch for Somebody will say just type it properly. Good answer, take it, then ask what the search box is supposed to do about the thousand messages already sent.

Slicing · 3 min · card 7 of 32

Pull the name out of the string

How it goes
  1. Open from the hook: you already know how to take a piece out. Here it is again, and this time we watch it happen.
  2. Press 1. Seven boxes and their index numbers, and an eighth box at the end that is greyed out. Ask: the name sits in four boxes. Say their numbers. Wait for 3, 4, 5, 6.
  3. Press 2. A dashed window lands over those four, and the grey box 7 is labelled never reached. Say it: seven is written down, and seven is never touched. Same rule as the recap.
  4. Press 3. The four characters are copied out into the box on the right. Say the thing the picture is doing: copied, not moved. Look at the row. message still holds all 7 characters.
  5. Press 4. The line appears. Read it left to right: message, square bracket, 3, colon, 7.
  6. Hand off: so that shape works when the characters sit next to each other. Watch what happens when they do not.
  • Four presses. The room answers before press 1 and before press 2.
  • Say the index numbers out loud with them. Reading them silently is where half the later mistakes start.
  • Worksheet and laptops stay shut.
  • Short of time: presses 1, 3 and 4. Press 2 can be spoken while press 3 is on screen.

Watch for A student may say the answer is Ravi without looking at an index at all. Ask which box R is in. That is the check.

Slicing · 2 min · card 8 of 32 · in a 6 min block

The one it cannot cut

How it goes
  1. Same shape as the hook, and say so: hyphens this time instead of spaces, but it is the same problem.
  2. Ask: can we get Ravi out of this with what we just did? Take answers. Somebody will try 1 to 8. Take it.
  3. Say what 1 to 8 would give: every character from 1 to 7, hyphens and all.
  4. Hand off: so we need a third number. That is the next card.
  • Do not give the syntax here. The next card is the syntax.
  • Two minutes. This card asks a question; it does not answer one.
  • Short of time: ask the question, take one answer, move on.

Watch for If the room is silent, narrow it: which indexes do we actually want? The answer 1, 3, 5, 7 gets you straight to the step.

Slicing · 2 min · card 9 of 32 · in a 6 min block

start, end and step

How it goes
  1. Read the shape once, left to right: start index, colon, end index, colon, step.
  2. Point at the step and give it one sentence: how far it jumps each time.
  3. Work the boxes on the right out loud with the room: start at 1. Add 2, that is 3. Add 2, that is 5. Add 2, that is 7. Let them say the numbers.
  4. Hand off: let us watch that happen one jump at a time.
  • This is the naming card. Say start, end and step here and nowhere else; every panel uses the same three words and none of them redefines them.
  • Short of time: name the three numbers and move. The next card shows them working.

Watch for Watch for a student reading the step as how many characters we take. It is not. It is how far the jump is.

Slicing · 2 min · card 10 of 32 · in a 6 min block

The jump, one landing at a time

How it goes
  1. Press 1. The same string, in boxes. Ask: where are the letters?
  2. Press 2. The marker lands on box 1 and that letter is copied out.
  3. Presses 3, 4 and 5. One jump each. Say the sum out loud every time: 1 plus 2 is 3, 3 plus 2 is 5, 5 plus 2 is 7.
  4. On press 5, stop. Ask: the next one would be 9. Why does it not happen? Wait. The answer is the end index, one more time.
  5. Press 6. The boxes it never landed on go dashed, and they stay that way. Say: it never looked at the hyphens at all. Boxes 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 were never even read.
  6. The line and the answer, Ravi, are on that last press.
  • Six presses, and the room says the sum before each one.
  • Do not rush presses 3 to 5. They are the only arithmetic in the session and everything after depends on them.
  • Short of time: walk two landings out loud, then jump to press 6. Press 6 cannot go.

Watch for If somebody says the output is five characters, they are counting the start twice. Count the landings with them: four landings, three jumps.

Worksheet task 1 · 5 min · card 11 of 32 · worksheet task 1

Worksheet task 1, then the slice machine

How it goes
  1. Say worksheet tab open, playground shut. This is the first time the laptops open today.
  2. Read the three slices off the machine out loud: code[0:8:2], code[1:8:2], code[1:6:3]. They write all three down before anything runs. Give them ninety seconds.
  3. Level 1. Count hands for A, then B, then C, then D. Tap the option the room chose. Run stays locked until something is picked. Then press Run it and watch the landings arrive one at a time.
  4. Read the verdict out loud, and say what each wrong option would have meant. Every option, not only the right one.
  5. Levels 2 and 3 the same way, faster. They already committed on paper.
  6. Level 4 is new. Count hands properly again.
  7. Level 5 is the one to stop on. Ask before you run it: the start is 6 and the stop is 2. What comes out? Then run it. Nothing comes out at all.
  8. Close it: a slice that never lands anywhere is not an error. You get an empty string, and print shows you a blank line.
  • Five levels. Run is disabled until an option is picked; that lock is the activity.
  • This card owns its own arrow keys. Use the buttons on it.
  • Hand the worksheet out here and nowhere else.
  • Do not skip the verdict on a level the room got right.
  • Short of time: sixty seconds to write, and levels 1, 2 and 5. Level 5 is the one that cannot go.

Watch for Level 5 is where the room goes quiet. Let the silence sit for three seconds before you run it.

Worksheet task 2 · 4 min · card 12 of 32 · worksheet task 2

Type it, then change the step

How it goes
  1. Say worksheet tab and the playground only. Nothing else open.
  2. Step 1. They type the two lines and run them. Ninety seconds, and start the timer.
  3. Step 2. Frame it as a want, not a test: I want the letters, not the digits. Change one number. They write what it will print first, then run.
  4. Step 3. Frame it as your own idea: I thought a negative step would read it backwards. Try it and tell me what happens. Do not warn them.
  5. Let them react before you say anything. There is no error and nothing printed: just a blank line.
  6. Then the line that makes it stick: Python did exactly what you asked. Starting at 1 and going backwards, it never reaches 8, so it lands nowhere.
  7. Hand off: worksheet closed. That is slicing. After the quiz we start on the methods.
  • Four minutes, timer started by you.
  • Do not tell them the answer to step 2 before they run it. One changed number is the whole task.
  • Step 3 is the one that cannot be cut.
  • Short of time: drop step 2. Typing it once and the negative step are the parts they cannot skip.

Watch for Two or three students will get an error on step 1 from a missing quote mark. Point at the quote, do not fix it for them.

Methods · 3 min · card 14 of 32 · in a 5 min block

How it goes

  2. Press 1. Two lines of code and an empty output box. Ask: what does line 2 print? Wait. Do not explain the method.

  6. Press 4. The same line with 984a. The last character is crossed and it prints False. Ask first what it will print.
  7. Hand off: that is the method. Here is how the deck writes it down.

strip · 2 min · card 16 of 32 · in a 6 min block

Two numbers that are not equal

How it goes
  1. Start from something they have all hit: you paste your phone number into a form and it says it does not match.
  2. Read both lines out loud, slowly: the first one has a space, then the number, then a space. The second one is just the number.
  3. Ask before you say anything else: are these two the same or not? Count the hands for same, then for not the same.
  4. Then point at the output: False. Ten digits, ten digits, and Python says no.
  5. Say why in one sentence, and tie it to the hook: a space is a character. Twelve characters is not ten characters. Same reason the search could not find SORRY.
  6. Hand off: so something has to take those off.
  • Count both options aloud before the output is named. Most of the room says same, and that wrong answer is the whole card.
  • Short of time: count hands once, then show the output.

Watch for This is the strongest reaction you will get all session. Let it happen before you explain it.

strip · 2 min · card 19 of 32

The dot in the middle stays

How it goes
  1. Read the code: M dot Ravi dot, and we strip the full stop.
  2. Ask before you look at the output: how many full stops come off? Take the answer.
  3. Then the output: M.Ravi. The one at the end came off. The one in the middle is still there.
  4. Say the rule properly, and slowly, because everything after this rests on it: strip starts at the left end and comes inwards. The moment it meets a character that is not in the list, it stops and never looks again. Then it does the same from the right end.
  5. Hand off: and now watch it happen, one character at a time.
  • Two minutes. This is the single most important card in the second half.
  • Say ends inwards, never removes the dots. The wording matters; the room repeats yours.
  • Short of time: this one cannot be shortened. It is the rule the exit turns on.

Watch for If anyone says strip removes every dot, go back to this slide and point at the dot in the middle of the output.

The explainer · 3 min · card 20 of 32

Follow the strip

How it goes
  1. Say what it is: the same idea, one character at a time, and you pick what comes off.
  2. It opens on strip("-i"), which no slide has shown. Ask: write down what line 3 prints. Give them fifteen seconds. Do not press anything.
  3. Press One step, not Play. Stop on every step and read the caption.
  4. The two steps that matter are the two green stop marks. Say it out loud both times: it stopped. It is not going to look any further in.
  5. Then the step that is the whole session: the dash in the middle goes dashed and says never. Say: that dash IS in the list. strip never got to it, so it stays, and it stays for good.
  6. Now change the set. Press strip("-") and walk it again. Ask first what changes.
  7. If there is time, press strip() with nothing in it. Nothing comes off at all, because there are no spaces on the ends.
  8. Hand off: right. Now you do one on your own, before anything runs.
  • Use One step, never Play, in front of a room. Play is for a student watching it again later.
  • This card owns its own arrow keys. Use the buttons.
  • Three minutes. It is a reveal, not a lesson.
  • Short of time: one set, not three. Keep the one it opens on.

Watch for The never mark is the finding. If you say nothing else on this card, say that one.

Worksheet task 3 · 5 min · card 21 of 32 · worksheet task 3

Trace it before you run it

How it goes

  2. Press 1 is the task. Read the program aloud once, line by line, without saying what any line does.
  3. Say the rule for the table: one row per line. Fill a row only if that line actually runs.

Exit · 4 min · card 28 of 32

Which one prints nothing?

How it goes
  1. This runs whatever else is unfinished. It is the hard floor.
  2. Read the program out loud, by line number. Do not explain it.
  3. Say the question exactly as it is written: for which one of these does the screen show nothing at all?
  4. Start the timer. Two and a half minutes. Silence while they think.
  5. Count the hands for A, then B, then C, then D. Every option, out loud, before anything is revealed.
  6. Press Reveal answer.
  7. Explain every option, not only the right one. The three that print Valid all strip down to the same four digits; the answer is the one where a dash in the middle was never reached.
  8. Close with the sentence of the day: strip works on the ends. Always the ends.
  • One question, and it is meant to be hard. Do not add an easier one.
  • Count all four options aloud before revealing.
  • Never cut this card.

Watch for A big vote for option D means the room thinks strip takes one character off each end and stops. That is the misconception to open with next session.

Closing · 1 min · card 30 of 32 · in a 5 min block

What a method gives back

How it goes
  1. The second map. The two columns are the two kinds of answer a method can give you.
  2. Left: strip, replace, upper and lower all hand back a string.
  3. Right: isdigit, startswith and endswith hand back True or False.

Common guidelines

The shape of the second half

Every method after the quiz arrives the same way, and the order is the point. The line first, with an empty output box and the room saying what it prints. Then it runs, and the mechanism runs with it. Then the deck's own slide, which is where the definition lives. A definition read before the example is a definition nobody has a use for yet.

Never start a card cold

Every card opens from something the room already has: the message on the hook, the program still on their screens, the answer somebody just gave. If a card feels abrupt, the fix is one sentence at the end of the card before it.

Walk it in steps, and check as you go

Do not deliver a finished explanation. Say one step, then ask the next as a question. So the space is a character too, right? Good. If the room does not answer that, you have found the gap for free. Offer doubts often. It costs four seconds.

Say it the same way every time

Three phrasings carry this session, and a room repeats the instructor's words, not the slide's.

  • The stop is never reached. Not it goes up to the stop. Say it on the recap, on the pull-out card, on the jump, and on machine level 5.
  • strip works from the ends inwards, and it stops. Not strip removes the dots. A character in the middle is never even looked at.
  • A method hands you a new string. Not it changes the string. Point at it every time one appears.

How every vote runs

Let them think. Read option A and count hands, then B, then C, then D. Every option gets counted before anything is revealed. Then reveal, and explain every option, not only the right one. A wrong option nobody explains is a misconception that goes home with them.

Picking students

Call a group, never a name. Back three rows, what did you get? Rotate the groups so it is not always the front. Never single anyone out for a wrong answer.

When nobody answers

Count ten seconds in your head. Then narrow the question rather than answering it. Just tell me, is it two characters or four?

Why the worksheet comes before the keyboard

The playground is where they check, not where they start. A task typed into the playground first and written down afterwards is a transcript, not a prediction, and the whole value of the beat is gone. Say write first, run second every time.

The laptop rule

Laptops shut except on a worksheet, playground or quiz beat. The entries say worksheet tab open on the first step of a task card and worksheet closed on the card after it, so it gets said at every change.

Why the worksheet repeats itself

Task 1 asks for three slices. Task 3 asks for one slice again, at the end of a trace, after strip has changed the string under it. That is deliberate: task 3 is the check that task 1 stuck, and a student who gets task 1 right and task 3 wrong has learnt the shape without the rule.

The cut order, if you are behind

  • Card 6, the plan, to two presses. Saves 1 min.
  • Card 11, the machine, to levels 1, 2 and 5. Saves 2 min.
  • Cards 23 and 24, replace, to one read. Saves 2 min.
  • Card 30, the second map, to three presses. Saves 1 min.

If they ask

  • Can I leave the start or the stop out? Yes. code[::2] means from the beginning to the end, every second one. Show it if it comes up; do not teach it from the front.
  • Does a negative step work at all? Yes, but the start has to be after the stop. code[7:0:-2] reads backwards. Today's point is only that a negative step with a start before the stop lands nowhere.
  • Why does an empty result not give an error? Because an empty string is a perfectly good string. It has no characters in it, and print shows you a blank line.
  • Does strip remove characters from the middle? Never. It works in from each end and stops at the first character it does not recognise.
  • Does the order inside strip's brackets matter? No. It is a set of characters, not a word. strip(".,") and strip(",.") do the same thing.
  • What does strip with nothing in the brackets remove? Spaces, and the other invisible characters like a tab. Not dots, not dashes.
  • Does isdigit say yes to a minus sign, or to a space? No to both. Every single character has to be a digit.
  • Are the method names case sensitive? Yes. Endswith is an error. All of them are lower case.
  • Do any of these change my string? No, not one of them. They hand back a new string and your own is untouched, which is why almost every example has an equals sign in it.
  • Is there a method for one end only? Yes, lstrip and rstrip. Mention them and move on.
  • Why does the search really fail on the hook? Because the app is comparing characters, and "SORRY" is not inside "S O R R Y". Run it in the playground if somebody pushes.
